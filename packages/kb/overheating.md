# Engine Overheating

## What is Engine Overheating?
Engine overheating occurs when the cooling system cannot maintain proper operating temperature, typically above 220°F (104°C).

## Common Causes
1. **Low coolant level** - Most common cause
2. **Thermostat failure** - Stuck closed, preventing coolant flow
3. **Water pump failure** - Not circulating coolant properly
4. **Radiator blockage** - Debris or corrosion blocking airflow
5. **Cooling fan failure** - Electric or mechanical fan not working
6. **Head gasket failure** - Internal coolant leak

## Symptoms
- Temperature gauge in red zone
- Steam from under hood
- Coolant overflow from reservoir
- Engine running rough or stalling
- Warning lights illuminated
- Burning smell from engine bay

## Immediate Actions (STOP)
1. **Pull over safely** - Turn off engine immediately
2. **Do not open radiator cap** - Hot coolant can cause severe burns
3. **Let engine cool** - Wait at least 30 minutes before checking
4. **Check coolant level** - Only when engine is cool
5. **Call for assistance** - Do not attempt to drive

## Safe Checks (When Cool)
1. **Coolant level** - Check reservoir and radiator
2. **Hoses** - Look for cracks, leaks, or soft spots
3. **Belt** - Check water pump/alternator belt
4. **Fan** - Ensure cooling fan operates
5. **Thermostat** - Check if stuck closed

## Severity Assessment
- **STOP** - Never drive with overheating engine
- Risk of severe engine damage
- Potential for head gasket failure
- Possible engine seizure

## Professional Diagnosis Required
- Cooling system pressure test
- Thermostat replacement
- Water pump inspection/replacement
- Radiator flush and cleaning
- Head gasket inspection if internal leak suspected
